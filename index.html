About Me

I am an undergraduate researcher at the University of Notre Dame studying theoretical and computational astrophysics. My work combines numerical simulation, physical modeling, and scientific software development, with research spanning core-collapse supernovae, dense matter, compact objects, and gamma-ray bursts.

I am pursuing majors in Physics and Mathematics with concentrations in Astrophysics and Advanced Physics, and expect to graduate in May 2027.

Research

At Notre Dame, I use the GR1D hydrodynamics code to study the effects of a hadron-quark phase transition on core-collapse supernovae. My work includes equation-of-state conversion, numerical stability, shock dynamics, explodability, and the origin of proto-neutron-star oscillations.

During a 2026 REU at the University of Utah, I benchmarked parameter recovery across several gamma-ray burst afterglow modeling codes and developed a model-agnostic MCMC program for automated sampling.
